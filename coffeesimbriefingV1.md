1. The Core Variable Architecture & "Real Evasions"
Instead of macro-economic shipping container delays, your Python engine maps out the hyper-local friction points where real specialty coffee margins bleed out.

The Micro-Supply Chain & Roasting Multiplier

The Roasting Mass-Reduction Vector: When raw green coffee is roasted, it experiences a strict 15% to 20% moisture loss variance. If your sales system predicts demand for 500 lbs of roasted coffee but fails to account for the mass-reduction conversion in green bean ordering, you hit a structural stockout.


The Freshness/Degradation Curve: Unlike shelf-stable canned goods, roasted whole-bean coffee has a strict peak freshness window (typically days 7 to 30 post-roast). The simulation models product degradation where quality drops, forcing either a margin-killing discount or an outright inventory write-off.

Hyper-Local Demand Shocks
The "Sidewalk Blockade" Scenario: A sudden local municipal construction project blocks the main entrance or removes street parking for 30 days, dropping baseline morning foot traffic by 35%.


The Weather/Mix Collision: A sudden heatwave shifts the consumer purchasing matrix from high-margin batch-brew coffee to ice-heavy espresso drinks. If the shop hasn't programmatically adjusted its milk, alternative milk, and ice replenishment cadences, it incurs emergency localized courier fees to avoid turning customers away.
